A **scope** is a pre-defined piece of delivery work with a known shape and finish state — a new feature, an old framework upgrade, a new platform, or ongoing operations under a service level. Scopes are a shortcut to a well-defined brief, not a separate purchase.

Browse every scope in the [scope library](https://www.scrums.com/ai-development/scopes).

## Scopes are included in your plan

Every scope in the scope library is included in your [Delivery Plan](/ai-development), on **Core** and on **Factory**.

Adding a scope does not change your monthly price. It is delivered from your prioritised backlog by the same team, within the same workstream capacity. What determines cost is how many months of delivery your backlog needs, not which scopes are in it.

See [What's included](/ai-development/whats-included) for coverage, guarantees and commercial terms.

## Types of scope

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Outcome-driven sprint** | A bounded piece of work with an agreed finish state, such as an MVP live with users or an API serving real clients. Milestones are agreed before work begins. |
| **Pod** | A standing cross-functional team, such as QA, platform or commerce engineering, that works your backlog sprint after sprint. |
| **Managed SLA** | Ongoing upkeep under response targets: maintenance, OS updates, uptime, observability and release QA, month after month. |

## Add a scope

1. Choose a scope in the [scope library](https://www.scrums.com/ai-development/scopes).
2. Add it to your Delivery Plan backlog and set its priority.
3. Your delivery owner confirms the scope with you.
4. The team delivers it when it reaches the top of your backlog.

You can queue as many scopes as you need. On **Core**, one active workstream delivers them in your priority order. On **Factory**, several scopes run at the same time against the same backlog.

A scope in your backlog is a request like any other. See [Making requests](/ai-development/requests) and [Tracking delivery](/ai-development/tracking-delivery).

## Work that does not match a scope

Scopes are a starting point, not a limit. Work that does not match a published scope is scoped from scratch on the same terms, against the same plan.
