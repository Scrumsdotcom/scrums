## What a scope is

A scope is a defined unit of engineering work. It describes the outcome Scrums.com will deliver rather than the person or tool you receive. A scope runs on a [Delivery Plan](/ai-development/overview#the-delivery-plan): it is an item in your plan backlog.

A useful scope includes:

- The problem and target outcome
- Included and excluded work
- Required inputs and dependencies
- Deliverables and acceptance criteria
- Delivery stages and review points
- Commercial unit and assumptions
- Security, access, and environment constraints

## Scope lifecycle

1. Select a published scope or describe the outcome you need.
2. Add it to your Delivery Plan backlog and set its priority.
3. Your delivery owner confirms assumptions, dependencies and acceptance criteria with you.
4. The team delivers the scope when it reaches the top of the backlog.
5. You review progress on the Scrums.com Platform and approve deliverables at the agreed checkpoints.

Each scope belongs to one [AI Development service](/ai-development/overview). The scopes register on www.scrums.com lists every published scope with its service, type and domain.

Outcome-Driven Sprints are one way to execute a scope. Do not use “ODS” as a synonym for every AI Development scope.
