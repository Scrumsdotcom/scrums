A request is the unit of work on a Delivery Plan. Everything the team builds starts as one — a feature, a change, or a bug.

## Raising a request

Select **New request** on the delivery board.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | **Feature or change** for something new or an improvement. **Bug** for something that isn't working. Type drives how the request is triaged. |
| **Title** | A short summary, written as the outcome. "Let clients view proposals in the SEOP", not "Proposals". |
| **Description** | What you need, who it is for, and what done looks like. See below. |
| **Priority** | Low, Normal, High or Urgent. |
| **Epic** | Optional. Groups the request with related work. |
| **Attachments** | Screenshots, designs, logs, specs. |

A new request goes into your **backlog**. You can keep editing it until it moves to **To do** — after that, changes go through the comments on the request.

## Writing a description the team can scope

The fastest requests to scope are the ones that state the user, the outcome and the problem. The pattern your delivery team uses is:

**User story** — As a *[who]*, I want *[what]*, so that *[why]*.

**Problem** — What is happening today and why it is costing something. Be specific about the current behaviour.

**Goals** — What has to be true for this to be done.

A request that gives all three usually moves from Scope to Build without a round trip. A one-line request almost always comes back with questions, which costs you a cycle.

For bugs, include steps to reproduce, the environment, and what you expected instead.

## Priority

Priority ranks your queue; it does not add capacity. A plan runs a fixed number of active workstreams, so raising everything to Urgent only flattens the signal your delivery owner uses to sequence work.

| Priority | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| **Urgent** | Production is broken or blocked, or a hard external deadline applies. |
| **High** | Needed in the current delivery window. |
| **Normal** | The default. Sequenced against everything else in the backlog. |
| **Low** | Worth doing, no date attached. |

## Epics

An epic groups related requests so progress can be read at a theme level rather than a ticket level. Assigning an epic is optional at creation and can be added later.

Epics are how the **Epics** view reports: each one shows its item count and how far its items have moved across Scope, Build, Verify and Deploy. See [Tracking delivery](/ai-development/tracking-delivery).

## Handoff and locking

A request passes through three states of ownership:

| State | Who holds it | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Backlog** | You | Edit freely — title, description, priority, epic, attachments. |
| **To do / handed off** | Your delivery team | The original entry locks. Changes and clarifications go through comments. |
| **Work started** | Your delivery team | Locked. Material changes are raised as a new request. |

The request drawer shows where it sits: **handed off**, **team reviews scope**, **build starts**. Locked requests are marked **locked · handed off** above the description, and the original entry is preserved so the scope the team committed to stays visible.

If a request needs to change after handoff, say so in the comments. Your delivery owner will either absorb it or split it out — scope creep inside a locked request is what makes dates slip invisibly.

## Needs your input

When a request is blocked on you — a decision, a scope clarification, an approval, or access — the team flags it **needs your input**. Flagged requests:

* Show a **NEEDS INPUT** badge on the board
* Are counted in the **needs your input** indicator above the board
* Trigger an email naming the request and how long it has been waiting

The flag clears when the team confirms the blocker is resolved, not when you reply. Items stay listed until then.

Manage email notifications from **Settings → Account**.
